*The Innovator's Dilemma* by Clayton Christensen is about how old businesses get wiped out by new innovation. Eric Markowitz argues there are plenty of counterexamples — including the D'Ieteren family, who pivoted from horse carriages to automobiles to auto glass repair, and today own and operate Moleskine, the luxury notebook company.

His argument is this:

> The innovator's dilemma isn't really about change... The harder problem, the one that actually destroys companies, is an identity problem. It's what happens when an organization has spent so long doing one thing that it mistakes the thing for itself. The product becomes the purpose.

He poses several questions along the way, and I think it's important that everyone be able to answer them.

So here they are:

1. If everything you currently make or sell becomes obsolete tomorrow, what would still be true about you?
2. Who would still seek you out, and why?
3. What is the thing underneath the thing?
4. What are you protecting right now that might actually be worth letting go?
